PlantPulse

A living plant conducting a band. Two alligator clips on a leaf pick up its bioelectrical signal; an ESP32 streams the volts; this page hears the plant as four signals ranked against its own day (how lively, how settled, where it sits, which way it is heading) and a composer in the browser plays from them.

The rooms

drift is a slow breathing drone: held voices cross-fading through a harmony walk, a sub under the root, the plant's energy opening the timbre while it rings. It needs no samples.
piano seats a pianist drawn fresh every song (touch, rhythm, register, how they develop an idea) with two hands on a sampled grand, and about half the time a cellist. It plays the Salamander Grand Piano and the Philharmonia cello, fetched from their publishers by tools/fetch-samples.

The music

Nothing is scripted and nothing is stored: every phrase is generated, remembered for the song, developed (never verbatim), and the song's hook is frozen from its own opening. The harmony is a walk over tonic, subdominant and dominant with the plant leaning it home or away. The same decisions run the live SuperCollider engine at plantpulse.hook.technology; this is the free browser port.

Hardware

ESP32-WROOM-32D, ADS1115 ADC, soft alligator clips. About $15. Build instructions on GitHub.

Built by Anthony Hook. MIT; sample credits in NOTICE.md.
