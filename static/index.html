Every note you hear is being shaped, in real time, by the bioelectrical pulse of a living plant. Nothing is pre-recorded and the music never repeats. Calm days get sparse, patient music; lively days get denser, bolder playing.

How this works +

A living plant generates tiny bioelectrical voltages as it responds to light, water, touch, and time. We measure those voltages, then let them compose music.

The signal chain. An ESP32 microcontroller with a 16-bit ADC reads the leaf voltage in millivolts and streams it over MQTT. A small Flask server forwards it to this page. Everything else happens in your browser: the page analyzes the stream for energy (how lively), stability (how settled), center and tilt, each read as a rank against the plant's own last 24 hours, and tracks the plant's slow daily character: its weather.

The music engine. A JavaScript port of PlantPulse's SuperCollider improviser turns those features into musical decisions in real time. Harmony moves as a walk through tension and resolution rather than a loop. Every phrase is played fresh, then remembered briefly so later phrases can develop it in a changed form. The plant's energy shapes how densely and how loudly the music plays, and its weather sets each track's palette and pace. Two rooms: piano is solo piano, sometimes with a cello; drift is a slow breathing drone with no pianist at all, something to sleep to.

The players. Every piece draws its players fresh. The pianist is drawn first: touch, rhythm feel, how much space they leave, how they move through a line. The piano room sometimes seats a cellist, with their own vibrato, bow length and taste for rests. Their words appear under the title while they play.

The instruments. The piano is the Salamander Grand and the cello is the Philharmonia Orchestra's; both are fetched from their publishers by tools/fetch-samples and never redistributed here. Drift needs no samples at all.

Track length and tempo. Each piece picks its own length when it begins, five to eight minutes (drift stretches to ten), and plays as one continuous improvisation until the harmony walks home and closes on a cadence with a slight slowing, the way a pianist ends a piece.

The plant is the conductor; the trace behind the title is its signal, live.

What the plant is deciding with
Energy
— how lively the signal is; sets note density and touch
Stability
— how settled; a steady plant earns longer harmonies
Center
— where the signal sits in its range; pulls the melody higher or lower
Tilt
— which way it is heading; rising leans the harmony toward tension
Weather
— the day's character against its last 24 hours; sets each track's palette and pace
